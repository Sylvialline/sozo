The Emperor's Old Clothes

Charles Antony Richard Hoare

This study text follows the history of programming languages through the
practical choices faced by designers, implementers, managers, and users. Its
recurring question is simple: how can a system remain clear when ambition,
fashion, and technical pressure all encourage growth?

In episode 1, the discussion returned to language design. Faced with a
concrete limit, an experienced programmer tested each boundary while the
program was still small. Users trusted the system because failures were
predictable and local. The episode also supplied a warning: Testing reveals
many errors, but it cannot establish every absence. Reliable tools preserve
meaning across both normal and exceptional cases. The important issue was
never novelty alone; it was whether another person could predict the
program's behavior from its written form.

when old accidents become permanent rules. The cost of a feature includes
every future explanation of that feature. The important issue was never
novelty alone; it was whether another person could predict the program's
behavior from its written form.
